# Interior page quality

Inner pages are where a template stops looking like a demo. Use the template
as the source of composition and keep each page useful in its own right. A
route count is not a quality measure.

- **Articles**: a complete narrative of five to eight blocks with at least two
  subheads, one pull quote taken from the text, one detail figure with a
  caption, four concrete facts (where, who, numbers, practical note), credits,
  and a way onward (next story, topic archive, the issue it appeared in).
- **Topics**: an intro written for that topic, real counts derived from the
  data, online stories and a print archive with page numbers. Do not pad a
  thin topic with unrelated stories.
- **Products**: price that reacts to options, a gallery with more than one
  view, specifications a buyer would ask for, delivery and returns, and a link
  to the story the object comes from. Stock language must be specific ("31 of
  50 left", "Few left") and never invented social proof.
- **Subscription and pricing**: clear terms (what, how often, how long, what
  happens at the end), per-issue maths, a gift path with its own fields.
- **Forms and utilities**: say what happens after sending and how long a reply
  takes. No decorative fields. Confirmation copy must not claim that an email
  was sent or a payment taken unless a backend really does it.
- **About**: real people with roles and one specific line each, the working
  principles, where to buy.
- **Legal and help**: short, plain, accurate sections with a contents list.
- **404**: a sentence in the brand's voice, a way home, search and three
  suggestions.

Reuse the template's typography, palette, shapes and image direction on every
page. Richness comes from useful structure and distinct relevant images, not
longer filler.
